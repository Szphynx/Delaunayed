Physarum FDN.

A slime-mold colony grows a reverb: the trails the agents leave become the routing of an eight-node delay network.

What it is

Thousands of agents crawl a field, drop trails behind them and steer toward stronger trails. Where a thick trail forms between two of the eight delay nodes, that pair gets routed together — so the reverb’s internal shape is grown, not designed.

The field is seeded by your input’s own spectral energy, so the network reorganises around whatever is loudest. Press ▶ Play, then watch the map settle while the tail changes character underneath it. ↻ Reseed scatters the colony and starts the growth again.

Controls

ControlRangeWhat it does
Agents200–6000How many crawlers. More agents build denser, more connected networks.
Speed4–25How fast they move. High speed reshapes the network quickly and unsettles the tail.
Sensitivity10–60How strongly agents steer toward existing trails. Low wanders, high locks into hard paths.
Decay85–99How long trails persist. Low forgets fast, high builds permanent highways.
Spectral pull0–100%How much the input’s own frequency content seeds the field. At 0 the colony ignores your audio.
Prune0–60Cuts the weakest connections. Raising it clears clutter and leaves the strong paths audible.
Dry / Wet0–100%How much of the network you hear over the source.
Feedback0–95%How long the network rings. High settings turn it into a growing drone.
Tone500–16kLowpass over the wet signal.

Recipes

Growing cathedral

Agents high, Decay 97+, Feedback 85%, Prune low. The tail thickens as the network connects up.

Unstable room

Speed high, Sensitivity low, Decay around 90. The routing never settles, so the reverb keeps shifting.

Follows the music

Spectral pull near 100%, Prune around 30. The network re-forms around whatever frequency dominates.
