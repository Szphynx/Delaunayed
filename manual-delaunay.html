Delaunay.

A delay whose taps are points on a map. Move through them and the delay morphs; hold still and it freezes.

The idea

A normal multitap delay gives you a fixed list of taps. This one scatters them across a plane and lets you walk between them.

Each dot on the canvas is a complete delay voice: its own delay time, pitch, level, feedback, stereo position and tone. The dots are joined into triangles, and a red playhead sits somewhere among them.

Whichever triangle the playhead is inside, those three taps are the ones you hear — mixed by how close the playhead is to each corner. Sit near one dot and it dominates. Move to the middle and you get an even blend of three delay times at once. Cross into the next triangle and a new tap fades in as an old one fades out. Nothing ever clicks over; you are always somewhere between delays rather than on one.

That is the whole instrument: write a landscape of delays, then perform by moving through it.

Start here

  1. Tap the page once. Browsers only allow audio to start inside a real interaction. The first tap wakes the engine and loads a short built-in loop so you have something to process straight away.
  2. Give it your own audio. Load file accepts WAV, AIFF, MP3 and M4A, and you can drop a file anywhere on the page. Audio in takes your microphone or interface instead. Loop repeats the file rather than playing it once through.
  3. Press ▶. The source starts and the delays begin filling.
  4. Drag the playhead around the canvas. This is the performance gesture — everything else is preparation for it.
  5. Set Dry/Wet last. The dry signal always passes through underneath, so this only decides how much delay sits on top of it.

Nothing you do to the canvas is destructive. If a constellation stops being useful, regenerate it and you get a fresh scatter of taps to explore.

The canvas

The canvas is the instrument. Everything on it responds to the same handful of gestures, with a finger or a mouse.

Delay & Freeze

Two ways to read the same constellation. Switch between them from the transport while audio is running — the change is immediate and musical.

Controls

Delaunay comes in two bodies: a full desktop panel and a compact device layout with tabbed pages. The controls below are the same on both; a few extras exist only on the full panel and are marked as such.

Transport

ControlRangeWhat it does
▶—Starts and stops the source.
Delay / Freezetwo modesContinuous morphing, or press-and-hold freezing.
Drifton / offHands the playhead to a slow automatic sweep so the delay keeps moving while your hands are elsewhere. Touching the canvas takes back control.
Regenerate—Scatters a completely new constellation. The fastest way to find a delay you would not have designed.
½× / 1× / 2×three settingsHalves or doubles every delay time at once without disturbing their relationships. Instant double-time or half-time feel.

Mix

ControlRangeWhat it does
Dry/Wet0–100%How much delay sits over the source. The dry signal is never removed, so this is a balance, not a blend-to-silence.
Feedback0–90%Master control over how much everything repeats, scaling each tap’s own feedback. Past 70% the field starts to sustain on its own.
Glide0–800 msHow quickly the instrument follows your moves. Short is immediate and can zipper on fast drags; long smears every change into a slow morph. This is what makes playhead sweeps sound liquid.
Width0–150%Stereo spread of the whole constellation. At 0 everything collapses to the centre; past 100 the taps push wider than the taps themselves are panned.
Output0–150%Master level after the limiter.
Tone300 Hz–18 kHzLowpass over the delayed signal. Rolling it back is the quickest way to push repeats behind the source.
Tempo40–220 BPMSets what each tap’s note division means in real time.
Taps6–20How many delay voices the constellation holds. More taps means denser triangles and shorter distances between characters.

Effects on a tap

You can insert one effect on a single tap rather than across the whole mix — so one region of the map is ring-modulated and the rest is clean. Each has a single knob.

EffectKnobSound
PhaserRateHollow sweeping movement. Slow rates breathe under a pad; fast rates wobble.
Ring ModFreqMetallic and inharmonic. Low frequencies add growl, high ones turn pitched material into bells and clangs.
GrainSizeA short smeared echo inside the tap. Small sizes shimmer, larger ones blur it into a cloud.

Sounds to try

Dub delay that never repeats itself

Delay mode, Feedback around 60%, Tone rolled back to about 4 kHz, Glide long (300 ms+). Switch Drift on and leave it — the tap blend keeps moving, so the echo pattern never settles into a loop.

Chord out of one note

Pick three taps that sit in the same triangle and pitch them to +7, +12 and −5 semitones on the TAP settings. Park the playhead in the middle of that triangle and every note you feed in arrives as a chord.

Hand-played stutter

Freeze mode, Freeze len around 90 ms, Freeze F/B near 50%. Hold and drag slowly across the canvas — each region you cross grabs a different slice, so the stutter changes pitch and tone as you move.

Wide bloom on a vocal

Width to 130%, master Feedback around 55%, and a handful of taps pitched up an octave with their Tone pulled down. Keep Dry/Wet low — around 30% — so the bloom sits behind the take rather than on top of it.

Half-time drop

Set up any patch you like in Delay mode, then hit 2× on the downbeat. Every tap doubles its time together, so the whole delay field drops into half-time without losing its shape. ½× does the reverse for a lift.
