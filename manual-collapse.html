The idea

Wave Function Collapse is a pattern solver: give it rules about which things may sit next to which, and it fills a grid so every neighbour is legal. Here the grid is a rhythm and the things are ways of shaping your audio.

The canvas is steps across time and lanes of audio stacked up the side. A playhead sweeps the steps in tempo. Wherever it meets a coloured cell, that lane fires.

You can paint every cell by hand like a step sequencer. The interesting part is that you don’t have to: hit Collapse and the solver fills the grid for you — not at random, but obeying rules about which shapes may follow which. The result is a pattern you didn’t write that still holds together, and you can throw another one every time the current groove wears out.

And the rules aren’t fixed. Paint two bars of something you like, tell it to learn from that, and every pattern it generates afterwards belongs to the same family.

Start here

  1. Tap the page once. Browsers only allow audio to start inside a real interaction. The first tap wakes the engine and loads a short built-in loop.
  2. Give it your own audio. Load file accepts WAV, AIFF, MP3 and M4A, and you can drop a file anywhere on the page. Audio in takes your microphone or interface. Loop repeats the file rather than playing it once.
  3. Press ▶. The playhead starts sweeping and whatever is on the grid fires.
  4. Paint some cells. Drag across the grid to lay down a rhythm; hold on a cell to erase it.
  5. Press Collapse when you want the machine’s version instead of yours.

Start on the Grain engine with the built-in loop — it responds to painting most obviously, so you hear what each tile does straight away.

The tiles

A cell is not an instrument or a sample slot. It is an envelope — the shape of that one hit, how loud it is, and how much of it spills into the echo. Six of them, and the difference between them is what gives a pattern its feel.

Rest

Silence. The playhead passes without firing that lane. Space matters as much as hits — a grid with no rests is mud.

Rise

Swells in, then drops. Reads as reverse-tape or a pad breathing in. Longer than most tiles, so it leans into the next step.

Sustain

Quick in, holds, releases. The longest tile — it overlaps whatever comes next, which blurs the grid into something more legato.

Fall

Instant attack, fast decay. The tightest, most percussive tile — this is the one that gives a pattern its groove.

Accent

Fall’s shape at roughly double the volume, and it pushes hardest into the echo. Use it sparingly, on the beats you want felt.

FB Junction

Shaped like Sustain, but almost everything it makes goes into the echo rather than straight out. This is the tile that grows tails — a couple of these turn a dry pattern into a ringing one.

Grain & Gate

Same grid, two different machines underneath it. This is the biggest single change you can make to how a pattern sounds, and you can switch while it plays.

Lanes have a fixed character. Lower lanes sit darker and further left in the stereo field, higher lanes brighter and further right. You choose where a sound sits by choosing which lane you paint on.

Teaching it

On the full panel, the solver’s rules can come from three places. This is what separates Collapse from a step sequencer that randomises.

ModeLearns fromWhat you get
Hand rulesbuilt in The default musical rule set. Good patterns out of the box, in a general-purpose way.
Learn from gridwhat you painted Reads every neighbouring pair in the current grid and allows only the pairs it saw. Paint two bars of your own groove, learn from it, then collapse repeatedly — every result is new but unmistakably in that style.
Learn from audiothe loaded file Slices your audio into steps, measures how loud each slice is and whether it is rising or falling, and maps that onto tiles. The rhythm of your sample becomes the rule set.

The workflow that pays off: paint a short phrase deliberately, learn from it, then keep pressing Collapse and keep the ones you like. You are not randomising — you are asking for more of what you just made.

Effects on a lane

One effect can be inserted on a single lane rather than the whole mix, so one voice in the pattern is treated and the rest stay clean. Each has a single knob.

EffectKnobSound
PhaserRateHollow sweeping movement. Slow rates breathe; fast rates wobble.
Ring ModFreqMetallic and inharmonic. Low frequencies growl, high ones turn pitched material into bells.
GrainSizeA short smeared echo inside the lane. Small sizes shimmer, larger ones blur it into a cloud.

Sounds to try

Chopped breakbeat

Grain, Grain length 60–90 ms, Feedback low. Load a break, then paint mostly Fall with Accent on the downbeats and plenty of Rest between. Raise Tempo for finer chatter, or press Collapse to have it rewrite the fill.

Ambient wash from a single note

Grain, Grain length past 400 ms, Sustain and Rise filling most of the grid, Feedback high, Tone rolled back, Dry/Wet near 100%. Feed it a held note or a pad and it stops sounding rhythmic entirely.

Gated echo groove

Gate, Step at 1/8, a sparse pattern of Fall tiles, Feedback around 55%. Then mute and unmute lanes on the level page while it runs — that is the arrangement.

Tail that never ends

Paint two or three FB Junction tiles, push Feedback past 60% and pull Dry/Wet down. The tails feed themselves, so the pattern keeps ringing after you stop the source.

More of your own groove

Paint two bars by hand until it feels right. Switch to Learn from grid, then press Collapse repeatedly and keep the versions that surprise you. Same feel, patterns you would not have written.

Let the sample write the part

Load a loop with a strong rhythm, press Learn from audio, then Collapse. The grid follows the accents and swells of the file, so the pattern locks to material you never programmed.
