How they depend on each other

  • Trunk sets timing, Decay sets loudness. They are independent — changing one never affects the other. Trunk is the only knob that moves the gap between a dry hit and the first repeat.
  • Angle (TREE) and Spread (KEY) together produce the actual pitch interval between branches — either one alone does nothing audible; the pitch is Angle × Spread, snapped to the Scale.
  • Children × Depth sets the total branch count (1 + Children + Children² + … + ChildrenDepth). This also sets CPU load and how dense the tail sounds — 2 children at Depth 4 is 31 branches, 3 children at the same Depth is 121.
  • Decay and Feedback (EXP) both shape loudness by depth, and they stack: Decay sets a fixed, finite falloff; Feedback adds real regeneration on top of it.
  • Wind Amount moves both pitch and time together, not one or the other, because a branch's heading is both its pitch source and (via bending) its delay time.

What's different from a normal delay

  • Taps aren't independent. Every branch reads its parent's output, not the dry input, so times, pitch and level all compound down the tree. A normal multitap's taps are siblings; here they're a family tree.
  • Pitch is structural, not a per-tap setting. There's no per-branch pitch knob — pitch comes from the turn angle (Angle × Spread) and is always snapped to a scale (Scale/Root), so the tree can't produce a wrong note no matter how it's set.
  • No real feedback by default. Decay fakes the sound of feedback (repeats getting quieter) with a fixed, finite falloff instead of a loop, so it can't run away or self-oscillate. Actual feedback exists, but it's opt-in and isolated on the EXP tab.
  • The whole tree can move. Wind bends every branch's pitch and timing together in real time, and dragging the tree directly is a live performance control — a normal delay's parameters don't move on their own.
