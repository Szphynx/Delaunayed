Getting started

Both instruments process audio you give them — they make no sound on their own. Feed one a loop or your microphone, then press play.

  1. Open a device. Collapse and Delaunay each run as their own page from the index.
  2. Tap anywhere once. Browsers only let audio start inside a real interaction, so the first tap wakes the engine and loads the built-in loop.
  3. Choose a source. Load file takes WAV, AIFF, MP3 or M4A — you can also drag a file straight onto the page. Audio in uses your microphone instead. Loop repeats the loaded file rather than playing it once.
  4. Press ▶. The source starts and the device begins processing it.
  5. Set the mix. Dry/Wet on the MIX page decides how much processed sound you hear. The dry signal always passes underneath, so at 0% you hear the source untouched.

Collapse

What it is

A grid of steps across time and lanes of audio. A playhead sweeps the steps in tempo; wherever it meets a coloured tile, that lane fires. Painting the grid is how you write the rhythm.

A tile is not an instrument — it's an envelope. It decides the shape of that hit, how loud it is, and how much of it feeds the echo bus. Six tiles, six characters:

Rest

Silence. The playhead passes without firing that lane.

Rise

Swells in, then drops. Longer than most tiles — good for pads and reverse-tape feels.

Sustain

Quick in, holds, then releases. The longest tile — it will overlap the next step and blur the grid.

Fall

Instant attack, quick decay. The tightest, most percussive tile.

Accent

Fall's shape at double the volume, with the strongest push into the echo. Use it on downbeats.

FB Junction

Sustain's shape, but nearly all of it goes to the echo bus instead of straight out. This is the tile that builds tails and self-sustaining loops.

The two engines

The GRN and GATE buttons in the transport are not a tone control — they are two completely different ways of turning your audio into sound.

Lanes have a fixed character. Lower lanes sit darker and further left, higher lanes brighter and further right, spread across the stereo field. You don't set this per lane — choose which lane you paint on to place a sound in the mix.

Delaunay

What it is

A constellation of delay taps scattered across a plane. A playhead sits among them, and the three taps around it are blended by how close it is to each. Move the playhead and the delay morphs continuously.

Every dot is a full delay voice with its own time, pitch, level, feedback, stereo position and tone. Drag the playhead through the field and you glide between them; there are no steps and nothing switches abruptly.

Insert FX

Both devices let you drop one effect onto a single lane or tap — not the whole mix. Add one from the LANE (Collapse) or FX (Delaunay) page; each has a single knob.

EffectKnobSound
PhaserRateSweeping, hollow movement. Slow rates breathe; fast rates wobble.
Ring ModFreqMetallic, bell-like and inharmonic. Low frequencies add growl, high ones turn pitched material into clangs.
GrainSizeA short smeared echo that thickens the voice. Small sizes shimmer, larger ones blur.

Recipes

Rhythmic stutter

Collapse, GRN. Short Grain (around 60–90 ms), Feedback low, paint mostly Fall and Accent tiles with plenty of rest between them. Raise Tempo for finer chatter.

Ambient wash

Collapse, GRN. Long Grain (400 ms+), Sustain and Rise tiles filling most of the grid, Feedback high, Tone rolled back. Dry/Wet near 100%.

Gated echo groove

Collapse, GATE. Step at 1/8 or 1/4, Fall tiles on a sparse pattern, Feedback around 50–60%. Mute lanes on the LVL page to open the pattern up while it plays.

Endless tail

Collapse. Paint a few FB Junction tiles, push Feedback past 60% and lower Dry/Wet. The tails feed themselves and keep ringing after the source stops.

Navigable delay sweep

Delaunay, DLY. Turn Glide up to around 200 ms, then drag the playhead slowly across the field — delay times and pitches morph as you cross each triangle. Switch on DRIFT to let it wander on its own.

Freeze stutter

Delaunay, FRZ. Freeze len short (60–150 ms), Freeze F/B around 50%. Hold on the canvas and drag — each cell you cross latches a different frozen loop.
